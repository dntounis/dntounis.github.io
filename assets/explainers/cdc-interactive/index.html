Drift clock

0.000 μs

The particles cross the whole chamber in ~3 ns; the ionization they leave needs up to 3.7 μs to drift the 29.5 mm half-cell at 7.9 μm/ns. Replay is slowed ~105× so the two time scales are visible: tracks flash, then hits latch one by one as their charge arrives at a wire.

Stereo shift: with the exaggeration toggle on, U hits displace one way in φ and V hits the other, by δφ = tan(41 mrad)·tanλ (λ = track dip angle). Axial hits don’t move. Measuring that U/V split is how the chamber gets z along the track — the real shift is ~40× smaller than drawn.

Cell readout

Axial (A) cell: wires strung parallel to the beam. The wire sits at the same φ for every z — a hit gives a precise r–φ point but carries no z information from the drift time alone.
Stereo (U/V) cell: the whole cell is strung skewed by ±41 mrad, so its transverse position slides by z·tan(41 mrad) ≈ 41 μm per mm of z — ±37 mm at the endplates, more than half a cell width. Move the z slider: the cell (solid) slides past its z = 0 outline (dashed) while the track stays put, so the measured drift distance changes with z. Comparing stereo and axial measurements of the same track solves for z.

Estimator

Why the ratio measures z. The avalanche injects charge Q at position z on a resistive wire (tungsten, 337.5 Ω over 1.8 m, terminated by 30 Ω preamps: ρ = Rin/Rw = 0.089). The wire is a current divider: each end receives charge in inverse proportion to the resistance it sees, so QR/Q = (z/L + ρ)/(1 + 2ρ) — linear in z. Invert it and you have ẑ.
Why the pulse shapes differ. Both ends see the same avalanche current, so to first order the pulses are copies scaled by the division ratio — the areas carry the z information. Second order: the wire is an RC line, so the pulse reaching the far end is slightly dispersed (slower rise, lower peak, same area). That is why SLD divides integrated charge, which ignores dispersion, rather than pulse heights.
What limits it. Johnson noise of the wire itself: σQ ≈ 3.3 fC per end (0.45 μs gate) on a 0.67 pC hit → σz ≈ 7.4 mm = 0.41% of the wire — the benchmark tool’s number. Fire a burst to see the scatter build that band.

TDC readout

Where t0 comes from. The SLC delivers beam crossings at known times, so the accelerator itself provides the start signal. The chamber measures the stop: the waveform sampler (200 MHz, 5 ns bins) watches each wire and marks when the summed pulse first crosses threshold (~3 electrons).
Time → distance. In this gas vd is constant, so dˆ = vd·(tstop − t0). What smears it: the first cluster isn’t exactly at the point of closest approach (ionization statistics, ~48 clusters/cm), diffusion adds σ = 68√d μm, and the 5 ns sampling contributes ~10–13 μm. Fire repeatedly and watch the residual strip build up a σ of a few tens of μm — the 100 μm budget of the real chamber includes alignment and field distortions on top.
Left–right ambiguity. A drift time alone cannot tell which side of the wire the track passed (dashed mirror track). SLD resolves it with the staggered guard wires, the 5° cell tilt, and the track fit across 8 wires.
